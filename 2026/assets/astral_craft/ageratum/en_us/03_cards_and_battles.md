# Cards and Battles

## Battle Preparation

When a fight starts — a Monster square, or meeting another player — the **Battle Preparation** screen opens. It shows:

| Element | Meaning |
|:---|:---|
| **Attacker** / **Defender** | Which side you are on |
| **Cost** | What playing a card costs you |
| **Ready** | Locks in your choice |

Both sides prepare, then the round resolves. **Which side you are on matters**, because attack and defense cards do different work depending on your role.

## Attack and defense come in three grades

The most common cards are graded, and the grade is in the name:

| Card | Grade |
|:---|:---|
| Attack (M) / Attack (L) / Attack (G) | Three attack tiers |
| Defense (M) / Defense (L) / Defense (G) | Three defense tiers |

::: tip
**Hold a spread of grades rather than only the biggest.** Cards have a Cost, so the highest grade is not always playable — a hand of only heavy cards can leave you unable to act. A mixed hand lets you always put *something* down.
:::

## Most cards only work in a fight

Cards are marked as combat-only or not. **The attack and defense cards are combat-only** — they do nothing while you are simply walking the board. Others can be used outside a fight.

So a hand that looks strong may be entirely idle between battles; that is expected, not a bug.

## Targeting and range

Each card declares how it picks targets and how far it reaches. Some need no target at all — the straightforward attack and defense cards fall in this group, which is part of why they are the reliable backbone of a deck.

## The rest of the deck

Beyond attack and defense there are dozens of named cards. A sample of what is in there:

| Card | |
|:---|:---|
| **Barrier**, **Barricade**, **Enhanced Barricade** | Protection |
| **Berserk**, **Charge**, **Blast**, **Bite** | Aggression |
| **Dragon Roar**, **Ashen Feather**, **Colourful Feather** | Themed effects |
| **Brick**, **Demolition**, **Entrapment** | Disruption |
| **Chocolate Cake**, **Energy Bar**, **Expired Bento** | Food — not all of it good for you |
| **Cheer up**, **Guidance of Fate**, **Directed Boost** | Support |
| **All or Nothing**, **Eye for an Eye**, **What you have, I have.** | High-risk swings |

::: info
**The card names tell you the design.** "Expired Bento" next to "Chocolate Cake" and "All or Nothing" next to "Eye for an Eye" is a deck built for party-game swings, not for careful optimisation. Reading a new card's tooltip before playing it is worth the second it takes.
:::

## Cards that become entities

Some cards visibly fire something across the board:

| Entity | From |
|:---|:---|
| **Card Arc Projectile** | An arcing card attack |
| **Card Laser** | A beam |
| **Card Falling Brick** | The Brick card |
| **Slingshot Projectile** | A slingshot attack |
| **Snowball Attack Projectile** | A snowball attack |
| **Gao-Sheng Cannon Projectile** | The Cannon square's shot |
| **Soul Link** | A link between combatants |

These are real projectiles with their own flight, so **positioning is not purely decorative** during a fight.

## Character skills in a fight

Your character's active skill is separate from your hand — it is on the **Use Character Skill** key and has its own cooldown. Five skill effects exist:

| Effect | |
|:---|:---|
| **Attack Boost** | Raises attack |
| **Defense Shield** | Raises defense |
| **Shadow Cloak** | Concealment |
| **Snack Time** | A food-themed effect |
| **Character Skill Status** | The generic marker |

::: tip
**Skill cooldowns differ between monster fights and player duels.** Plan a duel around your PVP cooldown, not the PVE number you got used to while walking the board. See [Characters](04_characters).
:::
