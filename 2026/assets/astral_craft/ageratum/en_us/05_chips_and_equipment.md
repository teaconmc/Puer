# Chips and Equipment

**Chips** are your equipment. They raise your stats permanently and are bought at the **Chip Shop** square (`platform_relic`).

## Three rarity tiers

Every chip has a rarity, and the tiers are colour-named:

| Rarity | Position |
|:---|:---|
| **Blue** | Basic |
| **Purple** | Middle |
| **Gold** | Best |

## The tiers are a real power jump, not a small step

Take the boxing gloves, which exist at all three tiers:

| Chip | Rarity | Attack |
|:---|:---|---:|
| Boxing Gloves (Basic) | Blue | **+1** |
| Boxing Gloves (Fair) | Purple | **+2** |
| Boxing Gloves (Excellent) | Gold | **+5** |

::: tip
**Look at that curve: 1 → 2 → 5.** Gold is not a 50% improvement over purple — it is more than double. Blue to gold is a **fivefold** jump on the same item.

The practical consequence: **saving for one gold chip usually beats buying several blue ones.** If you can reach a Chip Shop with real money, hold out for the top tier rather than filling slots cheaply.
:::

## Chips are themed sets

Chips come in families — the same object at different qualities. Beyond boxing gloves there are items like the **motorcycle helmet** line, and each family targets a particular stat.

Since a chip's stats are listed on the item, you can see exactly what a purchase gives you before you commit.

## Buying route matters

The Chip Shop is one square among 22, so reaching it takes planning:

1. **Get money first** — Windfall Hits, Lottery and Fortune squares are the income side.
2. **Route toward `platform_relic`**, not just any Shop — the general Shop is not where chips are.
3. **Use Haste and Portal squares** to reach it sooner; a chained Haste can save a lap.

::: info
**The Chip Shop and the general Shop are different squares with different names.** In the board list they read as **Chip Shop** and **Shop** respectively. Landing on the wrong one when you have saved for a gold chip is a wasted turn, so learn to recognise both. See [The board](02_the_board).
:::

## Chips versus cards

They solve different problems, and the difference is worth internalising:

| | Chips | Cards |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Duration | **Permanent stat increase** | Used and spent |
| When they help | Always | Mostly only in a fight |
| How you get them | Bought at Chip Shop | Card Bounce squares, hand draws |
| Cost | Money | The card's Cost value |

::: tip
**Chips are your long game and cards are your turn.** A player who spends every coin on cards will have a busy hand and weak stats; a player who banks toward gold chips wins the later laps. In a short demo circuit the card player may look better — in a long game the chip player pulls ahead.
:::
