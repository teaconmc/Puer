# Relics & Curios slots

Relics are the backbone of the mod: 67 of the 79 registered items are relics. They are worn in two dedicated Curios slots and mostly work by adding **attribute modifiers**, which the mod's event listeners then read at specific moments.

## The two Curios slots

The slot identifiers are generated by `ModCuriosSlot` and written to `data/minethespire/curios/slots/`.

| Slot ID | Size | Order | Validator | Entities | Accepts |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| `orginal_spire_relic` | 1 | 998 | `curios:tag` | `#curios:player_like` | the 6 *original* relics |
| `spire_relic` | 8 | 999 | `curios:tag` | `#curios:player_like` | the other 61 relics |

Both slots use the default icon `curios:slot/empty_curio_slot`.

> **The `orginal_spire_relic` spelling is not a typo in this document.** The source registers the slot as `orginal_spire_relic` (missing the second `i` of *original*), and the generated tag file, the slot definition and the translation keys all follow that spelling. The English display name is literally `Orginal Spire Relic`. Any datapack or addon must use `orginal_spire_relic` verbatim; the correctly spelled `original_spire_relic` does not exist.

Slot membership is decided by the item class, not by a hand-written tag: `OriginalRelicItem.getCuriosSlots()` returns `orginal_spire_relic`, `RelicItem.getCuriosSlots()` returns `spire_relic`, and `CuriosDataGenerator` builds the item tags from those sets.

## Equipping

Right-clicking a relic in hand calls `CuriosCompat.tryEquipOrSwap`:

1. The first empty compatible slot is filled.
2. If no slot is free, the mod **swaps** with an already-equipped relic that shares a slot, and returns the old relic to your hand.
3. The swap position advances each time, so repeated right-clicks cycle through your slots. The cycle index resets after 5000 ms of inactivity.
4. A successful equip or swap plays `ARMOR_EQUIP_GENERIC`.

If Curios is not installed, right-click falls through to vanilla behaviour and relic lookups scan the vanilla equipment slots instead.

## The state-flag attribute

Most trigger relics do not carry their logic in the item class. Instead they add one modifier to the attribute `minethespire:flags_group_0`, an `ActivatableStatesAttribute` that packs up to 8 boolean states into one number using 6 bits per state (`1 << 6*index`, i.e. powers of 64). Event listeners then test individual bits.

| Index | State | Tooltip text (`attribute.minethespire.state.*`) |
|:---|:---|:---|
| 0 | `BURNING_BLOOD` | After a perfect victory, heals 6 Health |
| 1 | `RING_OF_THE_SNAKE` | When entered combat, boost Movement Speed by 30% |
| 2 | `CRACKED_CORE` | When entered combat, summon a lightning charge ball |
| 3 | `LIZARD_TAIL` | When you would die, heal to 50% of your Max Health instead |
| 4 | `AKABEKO` | When entered combat, obtain Vigor 8 |
| 5 | `BAG_OF_MARBLES` | When entered combat, apply Vulnerable to enemies |
| 6 | `BLOOD_VIAL` | When entered combat, heal 2 Health |
| 7 | `CENTENNIAL_PUZZLE` | When get hurt, gain Speed II |

Several of these tooltips do **not** match the implementation. The mismatches are listed in [06 Damage types & combat](06_damage_and_combat).

Notes taken straight from the source:

- The item ID is `bag_of_perparation` (with `perparation`), while its attribute-modifier ID is spelled `bag_of_preparation`. Both spellings are real and neither is a documentation error.
- `bag_of_perparation` and `happy_flower` are declared as `+0.4` with `ADD_MULTIPLIED_TOTAL`, which is +40 %, not +30 % or +4 %.
- **`bag_of_marbles`, `blood_vial` and `centennial_puzzle` all use the same attribute-modifier ID `minethespire:bag_of_marbles` on the same `flags_group_0` attribute.** Attribute modifiers are keyed by ID, so wearing more than one of these three cannot activate all of their states at once. This is a copy-paste artefact in `ModItems`, not intended behaviour.
- `happy_flower`'s attack-speed modifier is also registered under the ID `minethespire:data_disk`. It sits on a different attribute than the real `data_disk` modifier, so it does not collide, but the ID is misleading.

### Lizard Tail in detail

`LizardTailItem.tryPreventDeath` runs on `LivingDeathEvent` for **every** living entity and does not read the `LIZARD_TAIL` state flag at all. It simply tries to consume one Lizard Tail from Curios (or the vanilla equipment slots). On success it:

- sets health to 50 % of max health,
- grants Blocking Value equal to **2 × max health**,
- applies vanilla Resistance V for 20 ticks,
- plays `TOTEM_USE` and spawns 140 orange dust particles,
- sends a packet so the client plays the item-activation animation.

Only the health part is described by the tooltip; the large Blocking Value grant and Resistance V are undocumented in game.
