# Leftover Pages

Six Leftover Pages exist — three per species. Each is an item that, when used, opens a reading screen showing its text. They are numbered I, II and III, and they are meant to be read in order.

They are the mod's real content. Everything else is the frame around them.

## How they work

Each page item stores a translation key naming its species and number. Using the item sends a small network payload to your client that opens the page display, and the text itself lives in the language files — so the pages are **fully translated in both English and Chinese**, and a resource pack could rewrite them.

Using a page also awards the vanilla "item used" statistic, so they count as used items rather than as read books.

## The Mauritius Blue Pigeon, in three pages

**I — Discovery.** An expedition finds the carcass of an unknown bird at an illager settlement on a tropical island: pigeon-sized, silvery-white head, red tail, and striking indigo plumage. The accompanying biologist names it provisionally.

**II — Exploitation.** The narrator takes several gentlemen to the island, where they shoot "quite a few" blue pigeons, divide them up and eat them. The gentlemen take all the feathers, and one collects the **seeds from the birds' stomachs**, saying they come from an ancient plant. The narrator does not understand these fine gentlemen.

**III — Absence.** More ships arrive. Men with "fire-spitting iron rods" shoot the blue birds out of the forests and carry them away by the heap. They can no longer be found even in the island's most secluded forests, and the narrator is beginning to forget the sound of their calls.

The seeds in page II are not decoration: the pigeon's dig behaviour and its seed-diggable block tag mean **seed dispersal is a real mechanic in the mod**, and the page is telling you that the bird's ecological role was noticed and taken as a curiosity in the same breath as it was eaten.

## The Thylacine, in three pages

**I — Description.** A new land holds an animal resembling a wild dog but clearly distinct: tiger-like stripes, a hairless tail, a pouch like a kangaroo's. Having found no literature describing it, the writer names it the thylacine. It notes that packs of wild dogs chase them, steal their food and prey on them — "poor creatures".

**II — Denial.** A rancher scoffs that "that bespectacled lad" claims the tiger-striped wolves do not eat sheep. He resolves to rally every hunter and rancher for miles around to wipe out the monsters approaching their pastures.

**III — The notice.** A zoo announcement: due to a keeper's negligence, the zoo's most important exhibit, the rare thylacine, has died after prolonged exposure to the sun. The responsible staff have been dismissed. By way of apology —

> **Admission will be half price for one week starting today!**

That last line is highlighted in colour in the text itself. It is the only page written as a public document rather than a private diary, and it lands the sequence: page I is scientific wonder, page II is deliberate extermination, page III is an institution treating the last of a species as a customer-service problem.

The historical thylacine died in 1936 at Hobart Zoo, reportedly from exposure after being left outside its shelter. The page is not inventing its ending.

## The two arcs, side by side

| | Blue Pigeon | Thylacine |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **I** | discovery and naming | discovery and naming |
| **II** | casual consumption | organised extermination |
| **III** | quiet disappearance | institutional indifference |

Both begin with someone giving the animal a name and end with the same silence, reached by different routes. Read one species straight through, then the other, rather than alternating.

## The Memoir

A separate `memoir` item is registered and appears in the Tools & Utilities tab alongside the manual. It sits with the reading material rather than with the remains, which places it as part of this narrative layer.

## Where to find pages

All six appear in the mod's own creative tab and in the vanilla **Ingredients** tab. In normal play they are finds — excavation output alongside the remains — so a full set is a collection to complete rather than something you can buy.
