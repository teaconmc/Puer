# Blessings and progression

## Choosing a blessing

After clearing a room you are offered **one reward out of several** — the screen is titled *Select one of the rewards*. You may **reroll a limited number of times**, and the interface shows how many rerolls remain. Rerolling has an **experience cost**.

So each victory is a small decision with a budget: take a good-enough option now, or spend experience and reroll attempts hunting a better one.

Blessings accumulate as *Maze Blessings*, viewable in their own panel alongside a Maze Info panel.

## The ten blessings

| Blessing | Effect |
|:---|:---|
| **Bedrock Armour** | a permanent attribute bonus while you are in the maze |
| **Springy Hands** | a permanent attribute bonus while you are in the maze |
| **Boots of Travel** | a permanent effect while you are in the maze |
| **Dungeon Forcefield** | reduces damage you take by a percentage |
| **Holy Blessing** | grants an effect immediately |
| **Heaven-sent Treasure** | grants items immediately |
| **Victor's Emblem** | each room cleared heals a percentage of health on **all your golems — both in the maze and in your inventory** |
| **Post-battle Supply** | grants items each time you clear a room |
| **Eye of Truth** | **doubles room-finder rewards** and gives **+1 view range** |
| **Maze Insurance** | when you die inside the maze, you gain something after respawning |

Three groups are worth separating out:

**The permanent ones** — Bedrock Armour, Springy Hands, Boots of Travel, Dungeon Forcefield — apply while you are inside the maze and stop mattering when you leave. They are the compounding picks: taken early, they benefit every room afterwards.

**The per-room ones** — Victor's Emblem and Post-battle Supply — pay out on every clear, so their value scales with how deep you intend to go. On a short run they are worse than a flat bonus.

**Eye of Truth is the information pick.** Doubling finder rewards changes how much of the floor you can see, and view range affects the map. If you are struggling to find the elite or boss room, this is the blessing that helps directly.

::: tip
**Victor's Emblem heals golems in your inventory, not just deployed ones.** Golems are carried as items until placed, and the blessing explicitly covers both. That makes it far stronger than it reads if you rotate golems in and out of a fight — a damaged golem you pocket still recovers.
:::

**Maze Insurance is the only blessing that pays out on failure.** Everything else assumes you win. On a deep push where death is likely, it converts a wipe into a partial return — pair it with the Key of Tomb, which recovers up to nine items from that same death.

## The nine advancements

They trace a full run from arrival to the bottom.

| Advancement | Requirement |
|:---|:---|
| Welcome to Dungeon Infinity | visit the maze |
| First Contact | clear your first room |
| Map Room | clear an **elite** room — unlocks the full map |
| Central Command | clear a **boss** room — unlocks the next floor |
| Collector | clear **every room on a floor** |
| Explorer | explore 100 maze rooms |
| Veteran Adventurer | clear 100 rooms |
| Bottom of the Abyss | reach the **lowest floor** |
| Conqueror | clear the **large room on the lowest floor** |

Note how the advancement text confirms the gating described in [02 The maze](02_the_maze): the elite room's stated reward is the map, and the boss room's is the next floor. The two hundred-room advancements are separate — one counts rooms **explored**, the other rooms **cleared** — so wandering and fighting are tracked independently.

**Collector is the completionist trap.** Clearing every room on a floor is optional; the boss room alone opens the way down. Doing it costs time and resources for advancement credit and whatever loot the extra rooms hold.
